Apple has issued a major update to Apple Creator Studio, its subscription software suite aimed at video and music producers, adding an acquired effects library, cross-application editing and artificial-intelligence assistants across its applications.

The centerpiece is the integration of MotionVFX, a third-party library of professional video effects, titles, transitions and templates that Apple acquired earlier this year, into Final Cut Pro. Apple said the integration gives editors "rapid access to extensive professional video effects and content," including specialized rotoscoping and color-grading tools, Engadget reported. Final Cut Pro also gains a Content Hub carrying Apple-curated stock video, photographs and backgrounds.

The update unlocks clips from Final Cut Pro's primary timeline, allowing them to be moved freely, and links the suite's applications: an image on a video timeline can now be opened in Pixelmator Pro, with edits applied back to the timeline without re-importing. Both Final Cut Pro and Pixelmator Pro gain text-to-image generation and editing, letting a user circle a region of a frame and describe the change in words.

Logic Pro receives a new Liquid Glass interface and, in a collaboration with OpenAI, an in-beta assistant that can edit projects, begin songs and generate vocal tracks from text prompts, Mashable reported. Apple said the same chatbot-style assistant will work in Keynote, Pages and Numbers, animating slides, drafting text and building charts. New "Producer Packs" contribute clips and sounds from well-known producers, and Logic's Stem Splitter can isolate or swap drum parts.

The release coincides with Apple's launch of the M6 iMac and a redesigned 14-inch MacBook Pro, part of a week of hardware aimed at professional users. Some Final Cut Pro features require Apple silicon. Creator Studio costs $13 a month or $129 a year, with a one-month trial, and the new tools roll out to existing subscribers in November.

The suite, pitched against Adobe's Creative Cloud, is Apple's attempt to build a full alternative for working creators with a single subscription. The MotionVFX acquisition and the OpenAI tie-up deepen that push.
