Anthropic's filing for what would rank as the largest initial public offering in history is reshaping trading far from Wall Street: shut out of the allocation, investors across Asia are buying proxies on the artificial-intelligence company, from its listed shareholders to semiconductor stocks.

The Claude maker filed final registration paperwork with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday, seeking a listing under the ticker ANT that reports say could value the company at between $1.5 trillion and $2 trillion, NPR reported. Such a debut would surpass SpaceX's June listing, which valued that company at about $1.8 trillion. The prospectus did not set a share count or price range.

Japan and South Korea are included in the offering's international portion, but access "will likely be difficult given high demand and various restrictions," Bloomberg reported, sending investors in the region toward listed shareholders of Anthropic, crypto derivatives and semiconductor shares as substitute exposure.

The filing also exposed the company's computing strategy, and analysts read a possible challenge to Nvidia. Anthropic says it manages compute "as a single, fungible pool from multiple strategic partners," drawing on four chip families — Amazon's Trainium, Google's tensor processors, Nvidia graphics processors and Advanced Micro Devices chips — a structure built over years at significant cost.

"The real exposure could be Nvidia's pricing power" when customers can shift workloads between chip families, analyst Patrick Moorhead told Business Insider. Ashish Nadkarni of IDC said the multichip approach also blunts Nvidia's supply swings. Gil Luria of D.A. Davidson cautioned that alternatives do not erase Nvidia's advantages in performance, networking and software, and Anthropic still spends billions on Nvidia hardware.

Anthropic's founders, including Chief Executive Dario Amodei, left OpenAI over the view that safety should be developed alongside capability, a framing the company has carried into its offering, Fast Company noted. The listing is expected within a month, ahead of a long-anticipated OpenAI offering, and will test how public markets price the frontier AI firms.
