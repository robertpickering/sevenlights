Shares of CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren fell in early trading on Monday, then rebounded later in the day, after Elon Musk warned that the more than 200 specialist AI cloud companies known as neoclouds face "guppy-level death and consolidation" as debt financing dries up.

"As of the past few weeks, debt markets have almost entirely shut off access to money for any company that doesn't have substantial revenue independent of AI data centers," Musk, chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla, wrote in a post on X on Sunday. "That means the 200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation. Some will make it through, but not many."

In the same thread, Musk pointed to his own company's finances: "SpaceX closed $40B of debt for GPUs, but we're investment grade and have Starlink & launch." SpaceX reported the $40 billion debt financing for graphics processing units on Sunday. The company operates its own data centers and sells computing capacity, making it a direct competitor to the neoclouds, which also rent compute.

Musk was replying to a post by Chamath Palihapitiya, founder of the venture firm Social Capital, who laid out a grimer view of the data center buildout. Mr. Palihapitiya predicted "a lot of partially stranded compute and compute sitting in inventory in 2027" than currently forecasted, "bought and paid for but effectively underearning (below total cost of capital) or collecting dust." He added: "2027 will be a turbulent year because it will clear out a bunch of wrong/bad bets. It will also be a consolidation year for a few."

The neocloud stocks had already taken a hammering over the preceding month, with CoreWeave and Nebius down more than 30 percent and Iren down 40 percent. Companies in the sector have invested billions of dollars in data centers and Nvidia chips to meet demand for artificial intelligence, and investors have grown concerned about debt-fueled expansion and whether the buildout will generate returns. Others dispute Mr. Musk's assessment, arguing demand for computing capacity remains strong.

Monday's swings showed how quickly credit conditions have become the sector's central variable. The next test comes as lenders and bond investors price the next round of financing for companies whose revenues are tied almost entirely to AI infrastructure.
