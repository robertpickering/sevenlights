From Seven Lights, this is your Daily Brief for Monday, October twenty-sixth, two thousand and twenty-six.

Brazil's electoral court has declared President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva the winner of Sunday's presidential runoff, giving the eighty-year-old a fourth, non-consecutive term. The court announced that Lula won fifty-one point eight percent of the vote, a lead of nearly four point four million over his rival, Flavio Bolsonaro. Mr. Bolsonaro, the son of imprisoned former president Jair Bolsonaro, refused to concede. Speaking outside his home in Brasilia, he called the result inexplicable and said he would seek to annul the vote. The count followed a chaotic election day. Lula's campaign asked that voting be extended, arguing that some local governments had cut bus services to polling stations; Mr. Bolsonaro also sought more time, citing heavy rain. A Supreme Court justice extended voting by two hours, but some polling stations had already deactivated their electronic machines, and Mr. Bolsonaro said the late change could ground legal action. The result breaks a pattern that had held since military rule ended in nineteen eighty-five: no candidate who lost the first round had ever gone on to win the presidency. Mr. Bolsonaro had led that first round, forty-seven percent to forty-five, amid abstention that kept more than thirty-three million eligible voters away. Addressing supporters in Sao Paulo, Lula said the election was a defeat for denialism and lies, and thanked young voters who took to the streets to defend democracy. He declared that Brazil would not accept being anyone's backyard. The victory keeps Latin America's largest economy, with annual output of two point six trillion dollars, at a distance from Washington, which imposed increased tariffs on Brazil this year, and leaves Brazil and Mexico, the region's two most populous nations, under left-of-center governments. Electoral authorities are expected to certify the result this week despite the threat of a legal challenge.

In Venezuela, the exiled opposition leader Maria Corina Machado has returned to the country after nearly ten months abroad, and her homecoming poses an immediate challenge to the interim government. Supporters gathered by the hundreds at a sanctuary in the city of Barquisimeto, west of Caracas, shouting freedom. Her convoy then took eight hours to reach the capital because crowds lined and blocked the streets and highways. Tens of thousands greeted her as she entered Caracas. The fifty-nine-year-old Nobel laureate said the country was giving lessons to everyone, and dedicated her return to the reunion of every family and the freedom of every political prisoner. Her ally, exiled presidential candidate Edmundo Gonzalez Urrutia, said he too would return very soon. Machado slipped out of Venezuela covertly last December, weeks before United States forces seized President Nicolas Maduro in January. Barred from challenging Mr. Maduro in the disputed twenty twenty-four election, she left to receive the Nobel Peace Prize and has lived abroad since. Her party said she had attempted at least seven previous returns blocked by the authorities. Her arrival deepens the rift with interim president Delcy Rodriguez and with Washington. The Trump administration had reportedly opposed the return, believing her presence would complicate its relationship with the interim government. United States-backed talks on a political transition began this month without her, and President Trump has said the United States will control a fifth of Venezuela's oil reserves.

China's Communist Party has opened the fifth plenum of its Central Committee in Beijing, a closed four-day session devoted to party discipline, the anti-corruption campaign and political loyalty to President Xi Jinping. The state news agency says the agenda includes a work report by the Politburo and the adoption of a document aimed at deepening party self-governance. Mr. Xi delivered the report and an explanation of the draft. A long commentary in the People's Daily, published under a byline reserved for leadership-weighted statements, said the party had waged an unprecedented anti-corruption campaign, animated by a resolve to offend thousands rather than let down one point four billion people. The purge has reached record depth. According to a South China Morning Post tally, thirty-five full members of the two-hundred-and-five-member Central Committee have been placed under investigation or stripped of their legislative seats, many accused of disloyalty to the central leadership. The military has been shaken most severely. Following the downfall this year of the vice-chairman of the Central Military Commission and a commission member, only two of the commission's seven members remain, including Mr. Xi. A communique setting out the session's outcomes is due Thursday.

In space, the National Aeronautics and Space Administration has laid out its plan for the Artemis three mission, scheduled to launch in twenty twenty-seven. The mission will spend about fourteen days in low Earth orbit testing rendezvous, docking and habitation procedures with test versions of the two lunar landers now in development, ahead of a crewed landing planned for twenty twenty-eight. Four astronauts from NASA and the European Space Agency will fly aboard the Orion capsule. Three spacecraft will launch on three different rockets. Blue Origin will first send a test article of its Blue Moon Mark two lander into a circular orbit about two hundred seventy-three miles above Earth on a New Glenn rocket. Orion will follow on a heavy Space Launch System rocket from Kennedy Space Center, modified to fly without its upper stage because the mission stays in Earth orbit. The crew will dock with the Blue Moon vehicle and spend about two days evaluating its cabin, controls, life-support systems and avionics in space, the first crewed assessment of a commercial lunar lander. On about day six, a test version of SpaceX's Starship will launch into a lower orbit, and Orion will undock from Blue Moon and perform a second docking with Starship, expected to last up to two hours. NASA administrator Jared Isaacman said the mission will serve as a tiebreaker between the two landers, though both companies are planning lunar landing missions in twenty twenty-eight. Hurdles remain: New Glenn has not flown since one of its vehicles exploded in May, and NASA has no contingency for carrying the Blue Moon vehicle on another rocket. Launch is expected in the summer of twenty twenty-seven.

Speaking of SpaceX: Elon Musk says the company has closed forty billion dollars in debt financing to purchase graphics processing units used to power artificial-intelligence systems, with the equipment expected to arrive in the first half of twenty twenty-seven. Writing on the platform X, Musk said the financing consists primarily of callable debt, and that SpaceX is investment grade, generating revenue from its Starlink satellite-internet and rocket-launch businesses. Bloomberg, which first reported the fundraising, said SpaceX used a special-purpose vehicle to keep the debt off its balance sheet and preserve its investment-grade credit ratings, with Goldman Sachs leading discussions with investors including Apollo Global Management. SpaceX has expanded aggressively into artificial intelligence this year, calling the business Super Intelligence. The segment has become the company's largest unit, with third-quarter revenue rising ninefold to seven point five six billion dollars. Musk said debt markets had largely shut off financing for companies without substantial revenue outside artificial-intelligence data centers, and predicted widespread failures and consolidation among the more than two hundred so-called neocloud companies building cloud infrastructure for machine-learning workloads, with only a small number surviving.

In manufacturing, Audi says it will spend four billion dollars to build its first factory in the United States, in Macon, Georgia. Chief Executive Gernot Dollner made the announcement on stage beside President Trump at a campaign rally on Saturday night, saying Audi plans to bring its biggest and most premium sport-utility vehicles to be produced in Macon, and calling the plant a long-term investment because the company believes in the American market. The decision reflects demand in the United States for large sport-utilities, including the group's planned largest-ever model, the Q nine. It also answers the cost of American import tariffs. Audi's parent, Volkswagen, says it remains under continued high pressure from those tariffs. The Georgia plant may add to friction at home, because Volkswagen has begun cutting as many as one hundred thousand jobs and closing factories in Germany, blaming competition from Chinese carmakers and the cost of investing in electric vehicles. Construction timetable and employment figures were not announced.

A word on the United States midterms, a little over a week before voters go to the polls on November third. President Trump held a rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on Sunday for Senate candidate Mike Rogers, joined onstage by the rapper Nicki Minaj, who travelled with the president aboard Air Force One and led the crowd in chanting the names of Michigan's Republican candidates. She said this was a major turning point for the country. Mr. Rogers, a former representative, is locked in a close race with the Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, a former health department director, whom the president attacked over his Muslim faith. Mr. El-Sayed called the remarks Islamophobic. Former president Barack Obama campaigned with Mr. El-Sayed on Saturday. Both parties are pouring money into Michigan, widely rated among the contests that will decide control of the Senate. Meanwhile in Alaska, Senate candidate Mary Peltola and her family sustained minor injuries in a three-vehicle crash while driving to the Alaska Federation of Natives banquet, delaying the start of her eight-stop final-week tour. The Anchorage police are investigating the collision, and her campaign says there is no reason to believe it was politically motivated. She is scheduled to resume campaigning Tuesday with a stop in Homer. Ms. Peltola, an Alaska Native and former member of the House, is seeking to become the first Democrat to represent Alaska in the Senate in nearly two decades, challenging Republican Senator Dan Sullivan. On Friday, Mr. Trump ordered the Justice Department to sue to block certification of the Senate election if Mr. Sullivan loses.

In the National Basketball Association, Oklahoma City's Jalen Williams was carried from the court on a stretcher with a severe lower-leg injury during Sunday's one hundred eight to one hundred one victory over the Los Angeles Clippers. With one minute and fourteen seconds left, Williams drove from the left wing and was met at the rim by Clippers forward Derrick Jones Junior. With little room to land, his left leg folded beneath him and the ankle turned at an unnatural angle. He was treated by medical staff for several minutes before a stretcher was brought onto the floor. Teammates covered their eyes. As he was wheeled toward the tunnel, Williams sat up on the stretcher and gave two thumbs-up to a standing ovation. The reigning league most valuable player, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, wiped tears on the sideline and said afterward: just feel bad for him, nothing more than that. The Thunder released no diagnosis, deferring questions on severity to further evaluation. Williams, an All-Star and All-League selection averaging eighteen points a game, was a central figure in the Thunder's twenty twenty-four to twenty twenty-five championship, and his absence removes the second scoring option behind Gilgeous-Alexander for the time being.

In Formula One, twenty-year-old Kimi Antonelli won the United States Grand Prix from pole position at the Circuit of the Americas, his ninth career victory, and now needs just two points in Mexico City next weekend to become the youngest world champion in the sport's history. The Mercedes driver led from the start and held to a one-stop strategy while rivals went aggressive. Charles Leclerc finished second for Ferrari after climbing from fourth on the grid, and Max Verstappen third for Red Bull after an early two-stop gamble failed; he reported engine-braking trouble and called the car undriveable on the radio. The title was not decided in Austin only because Antonelli needed his teammate George Russell to finish ninth or lower, and Lewis Hamilton fifth or lower; they crossed the line eighth and fourth. A ninth-place finish in Mexico City would settle the championship regardless of the rest of the field.

In Spanish football, Kylian Mbappe scored in the eighty-seventh minute to give Real Madrid a two-one victory over Barcelona in the league Clasico, the first meeting between the rivals this season and the first Clasico for Madrid manager Jose Mourinho in thirteen years back in charge. Mbappe opened the scoring before the interval, Raphinha levelled after the break, and Mbappe's late finish, his one hundredth goal for Madrid, capped a move begun by Jude Bellingham and finished by substitute Eduardo Camavinga. Goalkeeper Andriy Lunin made a decisive save shortly after the equaliser to hold the lead. Barcelona's nine-match winning start in the league ended, along with their record of wins at the rebuilt Camp Nou, and their first dropped points of the season reopened the title race after a single matchday.

And at the box office, the horror film Clayface, built around the shapeshifting Batman villain, opened at number one with twenty-four million dollars domestically and forty-three million overseas, putting DC Studios back on track after an uneven restart. Second place went to You Can See Everything, the documentary on the Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, which earned nineteen point five million dollars in its second weekend, a twenty-five percent increase on its opening, a gain rare for any release. Made for seven million dollars, the A twenty-four release has grossed forty-five point one million dollars at home and is on course to become one of the most successful non-fiction films in history; the all-time record is held by Fahrenheit ninety eleven, with just over two hundred twenty-two million.
