Next: Google has announced Gemini 4 Carbon, a new frontier artificial-intelligence model that the company says leads rival systems at finding, exploiting, and patching software vulnerabilities. There is a catch, noted in the Ars Technica headline: you cannot use it. Google says access will be restricted to vetted cyber defenders, expanding in stages to approved partners through a program called Fairwind, which offers governments, critical infrastructure operators, and major technology platforms a path to advanced models. Google's claims rest on benchmarks. The company says Carbon was more than twice as successful as its predecessor at writing secure code, and that Google's Chrome team found it outperformed a large frontier model from a rival laboratory by more than ten times when generating high-quality patches. The gating is the story's sharper edge. A model that can find and exploit flaws at scale is also a model whose open release could arm attackers, so Google's answer is a managed program with approved partners. Whether that holds commercially depends on whether the approved pool moves fast enough for the organizations that need it.

Next: a federal judge in Boston has blocked the Trump administration from enforcing a rule that set a strict definition of "medically frail" under Medicaid work requirements. For now, the states need not apply the stricter frailty test, and the administration may seek review.
