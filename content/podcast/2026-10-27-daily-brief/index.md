First story: Israeli voters went to the polls Tuesday in the country's first general election since the Hamas-led attacks of October twenty twenty-three — a contest widely described as the most consequential in Israel's history, and a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Most opinion polls show Mr. Netanyahu's coalition losing its majority in the one-hundred-and-twenty-seat Knesset, while the fractious opposition bloc is also expected to fall short of the sixty-one seats needed to form a government — leaving coalition arithmetic the central question after the count. The seventy-six-year-old prime minister also stands trial on corruption charges in three separate cases. Polling stations close Tuesday evening, with exit polls and early results expected overnight.

Jordan's armed forces said the operation was conducted in coordination with Damascus. But Syria's government gave a contradictory account, its foreign ministry saying it had informed Amman of its complete reservation over strikes carried out without prior coordination, and that it had opened an investigation. Syria's Civil Defence said seven people, including three children and a woman, were killed at four sites in Daraa and Sweida provinces. Syria also closed the Nassib crossing, its only passenger and commercial link with Jordan. Three suspects in the church attack were arrested after a shootout in Karak governorate. The court called the government's claimed emergency, at least in part, a self-constructed one — noting that the lawsuit was filed less than three weeks before the November third elections, and that California had already begun processing millions of ballots. Judge Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee in the majority, wrote that he would have rejected the bid even on the merits. Judge Patrick Bumatay, also a Trump appointee, dissented, arguing the federal government must have the power to act. Governor Gavin Newsom called the ruling a welcome speed bump. Judge Jesse Furman found that the dismissal of Ms. Comey, the daughter of former F-B-I Director James Comey, violated the constitutional separation of powers. He rejected the government's defense that Ms. Comey could be dismissed because federal prosecutors exercise the president's conclusive and preclusive powers, writing that the claim flies in the face of nearly two centuries of Supreme Court precedent. Ms. Comey alleges she was fired because she is the daughter of a longtime adversary of President Trump, or because of her perceived political beliefs.
