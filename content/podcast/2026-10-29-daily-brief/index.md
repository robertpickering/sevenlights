### First story
First story: Apple has announced its first MacBook Pro with a touchscreen and an O-L-E-D display, and its top configuration costs fourteen thousand five hundred forty-nine dollars — the most expensive MacBook ever listed. The machine was shown at Apple's "Pro and Tell" event, in fourteen-inch and sixteen-inch models with the new M5 Pro and M5 Max chips and a display Apple calls Pro-L-E-D X-D-R. Base prices are twenty-eight hundred ninety-nine dollars and thirty-three hundred ninety-nine. The touch layer supports pinch, scroll and drawing gestures. The headline figure is the fully loaded build: with the top chip, one hundred twenty-eight gigabytes of memory and sixteen terabytes of storage, the sixteen-inch model reaches fourteen thousand five hundred forty-nine dollars. The storage option alone adds seven thousand. Memory and storage cannot be upgraded after purchase, which pushes professional buyers toward the costly configurations at the point of order. CNET attributed the jump in part to the A-I-driven spike in the price of memory chips and solid-state storage.

### Next
Next: Xreal has opened preorders for the Aura, a pair of extended-reality glasses built on Google's Android X-R platform, with deliveries scheduled for mid-November. The glasses cost one thousand two hundred seventy-nine dollars, and early orders receive a one hundred eighty dollar discount. Dual Sony micro-O-L-E-D displays create a virtual screen with a seventy-degree field of view and up to one thousand fifty nits of brightness. The glasses carry four microphones, run on Xreal's own processor, and allow control by voice and hand movement. The lenses dim across five levels, sound is tuned by Bose, and they weigh under ninety-five grams. A separate puck, housing the battery and most of the spatial-computing power, connects to the glasses. Gizmodo notes the price places the Aura within reach of Meta's headset line, and framed the launch as the opening round of a contest in mixed reality. TechCrunch stressed the wired design: the Aura is a tethered headset, not the lightweight all-day glasses the industry has long promised. The mid-November date puts it in the market ahead of the holiday season.

### Next
Next: The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate steady at three point seven five to four percent on Wednesday, a month after raising borrowing costs for the first time in three years and within a week of the midterm elections of November third. The decision was not unanimous. Ten of twelve committee members voted to keep rates unchanged, while Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voted to increase the rate to four to four point two five percent. Chair Kevin Warsh said the September increase was beginning to work, and while inflation remains too high, he pointed to tightening credit conditions as a sign the economy is moving in the right direction. He declined to say whether another rise remains possible this year. Rates last stood this high in the run-up to the two thousand eight financial crisis. The December meeting is set to be contested: the two dissents show a bloc willing to press harder on inflation, while signs of softening in the job market could pull the committee the other way.

### Next
Next: Oil slipped below one hundred fourteen dollars a barrel on Thursday, as traders weighed diplomatic moves aimed at preventing a resumption of United States military strikes on Iran. The turn followed reports that Vice-President J-D Vance reached out to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran continued to give diplomacy a chance. Qatar presented Iran with a proposal to end the war, which Tehran called unacceptable, while other mediators held a separate proposal for review by Iran's Supreme Leader. And the Houthis' campaign against Saudi Arabia is drawing outside powers closer in: Egypt's parliament authorized military aid to Riyadh, Turkey said it would seek approval to send personnel under a new defense pact, Britain is sending fighter jets for defensive operations, and Pakistan has sent about eight thousand troops, expected to defend Saudi oil infrastructure rather than engage in fighting.

### Next
Next: Spain has begun three days of official mourning after two high-speed trains collided in the central region of Castilla-La Mancha on Wednesday morning, killing sixty-eight people — the country's second disaster on the same rail corridor this year. The state-owned Renfe train struck the rear of a stationary train operated by the private company Iryo near the town of Urda, about one hundred thirty kilometers south of Madrid. Officials said one hundred seven people required hospital treatment, fifteen of them serious. Transport Minister Oscar Puente said an automatic control system failed along sixty-nine kilometers of track, and the control centre then allowed trains to resume in circumstances he called inexplicable, with the Renfe train travelling twenty-five kilometers before the collision. Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez called the toll unacceptable and promised the utmost severity if negligence is found. Opposition leader Alberto Nunez Feijoo demanded a complete and independent review of the network. In January, forty-six people died in a similar collision near Adamuz. The mourning coincides with a campaign trail — Sanchez faces an early general election on November twenty-ninth.

### Next
Next: A federal judge has denied the Justice Department a temporary restraining order that would have forced Los Angeles County to stop processing roughly twenty thousand ballots federal officials associate with suspected non-citizens, less than a week before the midterm elections. U-S District Judge David Carter, an appointee of President Bill Clinton, rejected the emergency request in a twenty-six page ruling, writing that the relief sought would hand the courts to the will of the Executive Branch, and adding: "This Court declines to be a party to such process." The suit rested on a Homeland Security analysis claiming as many as one hundred ninety thousand non-citizens sit on California's voter rolls. Los Angeles County's own analysis found the federal list unreliable, and the county and state argued that halting the counting would be unlawful and would disenfranchise eligible voters. Judge Carter noted the government could simply continue indicting people for the crime of illegally voting. The Justice Department said immediately that it will appeal.

### Next
Next: The New York Times reported that the campaign of Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola of Alaska worked to find other people named Dan Sullivan and encourage them to run, in an effort to split the vote of the Republican incumbent she is trying to unseat. The report, based on interviews with her former campaign manager, documents and text messages, said the effort produced a second Dan Sullivan who qualified for the ballot. Alaska election authorities tried to disqualify him, but the state Supreme Court ruled he could appear, and he advanced to the general election, where the senator's name will appear twice. Alaska's top-four primary and ranked-choice general election make such dynamics consequential. The Peltola campaign denies any involvement. President Trump called the report proof of election fraud and said Ms. Peltola should not be allowed to run. The Alaska race is among the handful Democrats need to win to take the Senate, and recent polls show Ms. Peltola in the lead.

### Next
Next: Dolores Huerta, the labor organizer who co-founded what became the United Farm Workers with Cesar Chavez, has died at ninety-six. She helped organize the nineteen sixty-five Delano grape strike and boycott — a national boycott of table grapes that won pickers better wages and working conditions — and gave American politics its rallying cry, "Yes, we can." Born in the mining town of Dawson, New Mexico, and raised around labor activism, she and Mr. Chavez founded the National Farm Workers Association in nineteen sixty-two. In twenty twelve, President Barack Obama awarded her the Presidential Medal of Freedom and acknowledged that his "Yes, we can" slogan drew on her chant. California declared her birthday, April tenth, Dolores Huerta Day. Tributes came from across public life: U-F-W president Teresa Romero said the greatest monument to her memory is for the union to keep growing, and former vice-president Kamala Harris said she was proud to organize alongside her. Ms. Huerta remained at rallies well into her nineties.

### Next
Next: A federal court has sentenced a thirty-six-year-old Ukrainian national, Artem Stryzhak, to seventy months in prison for his affiliate role in the Nefilim and Hive ransomware operations. Stryzhak had been living in Barcelona. Spanish police arrested him in June twenty twenty-four and extradited him to the United States. Court documents show he joined Nefilim as an affiliate in twenty twenty-one, striking organizations in the United States, Norway, France, Switzerland, Germany and the Netherlands, and agreed to pay the operators twenty percent of the ransom proceeds he collected. Around the same period he became one of Hive's earliest affiliates, encrypting a dental office, a healthcare provider and law firms during the pandemic. The F-B-I said these groups caused more than one hundred million dollars in losses. The Justice Department used the sentencing to renew rewards totaling up to eleven million dollars for information leading to Nefilim's administrator, who remains at large.

### Next
Next: Police in Greenville, South Carolina, have closed their investigation into the death of the actress Hayden Panettiere, more than two months after she was found unresponsive at her apartment complex. No one will be charged. The Greenville Police Department said that, based on the medical examiner's determination, her death has been ruled accidental, with no foul play suspected. The coroner ruled the cause the toxic effects of a combination of fentanyl, a fentanyl precursor, and prescription sedatives and muscle relaxants. It remains unclear whether federal authorities will pursue their own inquiry. Ms. Panettiere, thirty-six, was a child actor before starring roles on "Heroes" and "Nashville" and the film "Scream the Sixth." Legal questions over her estate continue: her former partner Wladimir Klitschko filed for temporary guardianship of their eleven-year-old daughter's interest in the estate, as the daughter is the sole inheritor, and a court granted it.

Next: Ernie Johnson announced that this will be his last season hosting "Inside the N-B-A" on T-N-T, the program he has led for thirty-seven years. He is seventy. "This will be my last season, totally my decision. It's just time," he said, explaining that if the show ever became more of a challenge than a joy, he would step away. He disclosed a contributing factor: a heart condition, with bypass surgery fourteen months ago, and he is still trying to get his heart to beat exactly the way it should. ### Next
Next: Early reactions to "The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping" are running strongly positive ahead of the film's release in theaters on November twentieth. The sixth installment in the Lionsgate series, directed again by Francis Lawrence, follows the teenager Haymitch Abernathy decades before the original trilogy. Reviewers singled out newcomer Joseph Zada in the role Woody Harrelson played in the earlier films — one critic called him a revelation, charismatic, lovable, tender and honest. Several comparisons placed the film among the best of the franchise, with some measuring it against "Rogue One" as a prequel that deepens rather than dilutes the original story. Jennifer Lawrence, asked about her return to the world she starred in as Katniss Everdeen, effectively confirmed that Woody Harrelson reappears in a cameo. The reactions matter commercially: the franchise's previous film opened to modest returns by the series' standards.

### Next
Next: Liverpool beat Chelsea six-five on penalties at Anfield after a one-one draw in the Carabao Cup fourth round, extending Chelsea's peculiar misery in this contest to five consecutive shootout defeats. Morgan Rogers put Chelsea ahead from the penalty spot in the fortieth minute, and Cody Gakpo answered from the mark three minutes later. Chelsea had lost the preceding four penalty contests against Liverpool, including the twenty nineteen U-E-F-A Super Cup and both domestic finals of twenty twenty-two. In sudden death, Liverpool missed the chance to end it themselves before Joe Gomez — who has never scored for Liverpool in open play — struck the first shootout penalty of his Anfield career into the top corner. Wesley Fofana then lifted Chelsea's attempt high into the stands, and the tie was over. Liverpool face Bournemouth in the quarter-finals. Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso called the result a small setback in what he said is probably the least important competition for his club.

### And finally
And finally: Alessia Russo scored all three goals as Arsenal beat Benfica three-no in the Women's Champions League, becoming the highest English goalscorer in the history of the competition. The captain's hat-trick — her tenth goal in her past seven matches — lifted Arsenal to second in the league-phase table and eased the pressure on head coach Renee Slegers after a start to the season that had drawn scrutiny. Russo opened the scoring just before half-time with a fine finish after a well-worked move down the left, rose high to glance home a corner after the break, and completed the afternoon from the penalty spot. "She's one of the leaders and she does it her way," Slegers said. "She leads the way as a role model, doing the right things consistently." Last season's semi-finalists hold an unbeaten home record in the league phase, with fixtures against Barcelona and OL Lyonnes to come. In the same round, Chelsea beat Roma, and eight-time champions OL Lyonnes won six-no over Internazionale.
